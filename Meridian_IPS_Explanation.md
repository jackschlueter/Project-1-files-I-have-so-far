# Meridian Foundation Reserve: investment policy statement

## What is an IPS?

An investment policy statement (IPS) is a written guide for managing an investor's assets. It records the purpose of the money, investment objectives and limits, decision authority, and how results and risks are monitored. The [CFA Institute reading](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/-/media/documents/article/position-paper/investment-policy-statement-institutional-investors.pdf) describes these as parts of an institutional IPS; its sample figures are illustrative, not Meridian's policy.

## How do objectives and constraints work?

Objectives describe the desired result and how to evaluate it. Meridian seeks CPI + 4% a year net of costs over rolling five-year periods (S2), while its 60/40 benchmark (S4) supplies a comparison. Constraints define which portfolios and actions are allowed: payout reserves, long-only investing, issuer limits, turnover approval, and liquidity (S3 and S5–S8). Check those limits first; then choose among permitted portfolios using the CIO's chosen optimization objective. A holdings snapshot cannot establish future returns or trading behavior.

| Statement | IPS element | Meaning |

| S8 | Liquidity limit | At least 90% must be redeemable within five business days. |
| S9 | Risk statement needing clarification | Define the loss measure and breach response before a 10% calendar-year limit can be used. |
| S10 | Preferences and missing objective | No stated sector, ESG, tax, or home-bias preference; the CIO must choose what the pilot optimizes. |
