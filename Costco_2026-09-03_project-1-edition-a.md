## Initial thesis

**Watch / defer — do not initiate yet.**

Costco has a distinctive, durable membership-warehouse model, strong sales momentum, high liquidity, and net cash. However, the present valuation embeds substantial execution and growth expectations. Before initiating, the committee should establish an explicit expected-return case using a defensible earnings-growth and exit-multiple range; this Edition A does not yet demonstrate adequate upside after a valuation margin of safety.

## Consequential assumptions

- Membership renewal, traffic, and comparable-sales strength remain durable rather than temporarily boosted by price or macro conditions.
- New warehouse openings and digital growth increase economic value without materially diluting returns on invested capital.
- Competitive pressure from mass retail, e-commerce, and club formats does not materially impair Costco's member value proposition or thin operating margins.
- The market continues to assign Costco a premium earnings multiple, or earnings growth is sufficient to offset any multiple compression.
- Enterprise value is used consistently with operating metrics; equity value is used consistently with EPS and net-income metrics.

## Unknowns

- A base, bull, and bear forecast for revenue, membership-fee growth, operating margin, capital expenditure, free cash flow, and share count.
- A justified intrinsic value range and expected IRR over the fund's intended holding period.
- Costco's current membership renewal and penetration trends by geography and cohort.
- Unit economics and expected returns for new warehouses, including international expansion.
- The sensitivity of valuation to margin pressure, slower comparable sales, or a lower exit multiple.
- Portfolio fit: position sizing, existing factor exposures, liquidity needs, and downside tolerance.

## Research plan

1. Build a three-scenario five-year operating model from the FY2025 10-K and subsequent 10-Qs, separating comparable sales, warehouse growth, membership fees, merchandise margin, SG&A, capex, and share repurchases.
2. Calculate valuation through at least two methods: a DCF using normalized free cash flow and an EV/EBIT or P/E multiple framework using peer and historical ranges.
3. Test the valuation under slower comparable sales, lower membership renewal, margin compression, capex overruns, and exit-multiple compression.
4. Review earnings-call materials, competitor filings, and management disclosures for unit economics, renewal rates, pricing, international expansion, and e-commerce profitability.
5. Present an initiation threshold: price or valuation at which the base-case expected return compensates for the bear-case downside and fund-level risk.

## Partner-generated falsification question

What evidence over the next two quarters would show that Costco's membership model is no longer converting warehouse expansion and higher member fees into sustainable per-share free-cash-flow growth?
