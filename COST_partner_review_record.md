## My presentation: conclusion first

This is a dated classroom scenario, not a current price target. I need to rebase the model on reported FY2026 results before changing the conclusion. [Linked model and submission](COST_Valuation_Submission.md) · [Dated price](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/cost/history/)

1. **Target selection.** I chose Costco because paid memberships, renewal, merchandise sales, and warehouse activity provide operating measures that can be traced into cash flow. My initial view was favorable toward the business, but cautious about what the share price assumed. [Company research](COST-research/Costco_2026-09-03_report.md)

2. **Company and evidence.** Costco earns revenue from merchandise sales and annual membership fees. Its FY2025 10-K reports $269,912 million of net sales and $5,323 million of fees; the FY2023–FY2025 10-Ks anchor the history. The May 2026 10-Q supplies the model's share denominator. Reported amounts are evidence; forecast rates are judgments. [FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983225000101/cost-20250831.htm) · [May 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983226000051/cost-20260510.htm)

3. **Pro forma.** The five-year model assumes 6% annual net-sales growth, 7% annual fee growth, 11.1% merchandise gross margin, 71% SG&A to calculated gross profit, and capital spending of 2% of sales. Paid-member growth is a company-specific driver. A changed member-growth rate scales sales and fees relative to the base; that one-for-one link is a classroom judgment, not a measured Costco elasticity. Forecast profit flows through working capital, capital spending, debt, and cash. The saved cases pass balance-sheet, cash, property, debt, equity, and minimum-cash checks. [Three-statement model](COST_three_statement_2026_2030.md) · [Audit](COST_proforma_audit.txt)

4. **Valuation.** The linked model discounts cash flow to equity at a judgmental 10% cost of equity with 2.5% terminal growth; no enterprise-to-equity bridge is needed for that equity method. The earlier enterprise DCF uses FY2025 starting FCFF, a **10% WACC placeholder** and 3% terminal growth, then adds cash and subtracts debt to reach $324.54 per diluted share. BJ's is the sole admitted membership-warehouse P/E peer, producing a $376.88 single-peer reference; Walmart is excluded because its public EPS reflects much more than Sam's Club. The methods have different cash-flow definitions, forecasts, rates, and share bases, so I do not average them. At the earlier DCF's saved **$902.75 September 9, 2026 after-hours price**, the reverse DCF has no solution inside the assigned −5 to +10 percentage-point growth-shift bracket. Holding starting FCFF, WACC, terminal growth, cash, debt, and diluted shares fixed, an unconstrained calculation implies 34.34% annual FCFF growth for five years. That is a diagnostic implication, not a prediction. [DCF and reverse DCF](Costco_DCF.md) · [Peer policy and result](Costco_Peer_Policy_and_Candidate_Log.md)

5. **Sensitivity and drivers.** In the saved Lab 11 footprint case, changing only average warehouse size from **147,000 to 148,000 square feet per warehouse** (tested range: 146,000–148,000) raises FY2030 operating income from $13,792.2 million to $13,871.3 million, cash flow to equity from $5,875.0 million to $5,902.3 million, and value from **$205.65 to $206.48 per share**. The model assumes the extra space lifts merchandise sales at unchanged sales per square foot. Higher gross profit raises operating cash, partly offset by more working capital and capital spending. The scenario does not separately cost enlarging the fleet. In another one-input range, capital spending of 1.8% or 2.2% of sales gives $220.99 or $190.30 per share versus the 2.0% base. Capital spending moves value more **over these particular tested ranges**; that does not establish a universal ranking. [Lab 11 results](COST_footprint_two_driver_sensitivity.md) · [Locked prediction and actual result](COST_locked_footprint_record_2026-09-29.md)

6. **Interpretation.** The work made my initial caution specific: the saved forecasts do not support the dated market price, and terminal value and the discount rate carry substantial judgment. New FY2026 results already show that the old forecast needs updating: actual sales, operating income, and operating cash flow exceeded modeled FY2026 amounts, while capital spending was also higher. My next research priority is a full FY2026 rebase and evidence for sustainable member growth, margins, spending, and a defensible discount rate. The current review does not claim that a model repair has been run.
