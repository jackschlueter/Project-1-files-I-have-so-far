# COST: company-specific operating driver

**Recommended main driver: paid membership growth**, measured in **millions of paid memberships**, with renewal and Executive upgrades as the supporting checks. This is an operating count, not a financial statement total.

Costco reported **81.0 million paid memberships** at FY2025 year-end versus **76.2 million** a year earlier, growth of about **6.3%**. It also reported **38.7 million Executive members**, a **89.8% worldwide renewal rate** (92.3% in the U.S. and Canada), and Executive-member spending equal to about **73.6% of worldwide net sales**. Costco explicitly says membership-base growth, Executive penetration, and renewals materially influence profitability. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 and Item 7](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983225000101/cost-20250831.htm)

The operating mechanism is: more retained and newly acquired paid members produce more annual membership-fee opportunities and a larger pool of shoppers; shopping trips and spending per trip turn that member base into merchandise sales. A model could express this as:

`membership fees ≈ average paid memberships × recognized fee per membership`

`merchandise sales ≈ shopping trips × average merchandise spend per trip`

These are modeling approximations, not Costco-reported identities. The published paid-member count includes a small number of recently expired memberships, and the count at year-end is not the same as average active members during the year. The unit measure and the fee yield therefore need care before a valuation rerun. The existing model separately assumes 6% net-sales growth and 7% fee growth; changing paid-member growth must replace or decompose those assumptions to avoid counting member growth twice.

**Why it is more useful than square feet per warehouse as the main narrative:** warehouse area is capacity. Costco says square-footage growth has become a comparatively less substantial source of sales growth as its warehouse base expands. Member acquisition, renewal, and shopping behavior explain how the space produces sales. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983225000101/cost-20250831.htm)

For a second operating measure, track **shopping frequency**: Costco attributed about **5 percentage points** of FY2025 comparable-sales growth to more visits and about **1 point** to average ticket. It is closer to a transaction-volume measure, but the filing gives the growth rate, not an absolute count of trips. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983225000101/cost-20250831.htm)

The latest FY2026 operating-results release, dated September 24, 2026, reports **$5.907 billion membership-fee revenue**, **$297.247 billion net sales**, and **939 warehouses**. That release can update historical anchors in a later forecast; it should not be mixed into the current FY2025-based opening statements without rebuilding the model. [Costco FY2026 operating results](https://investor.costco.com/news/news-details/2026/Costco-Wholesale-Corporation-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2026-Operating-Results/default.aspx)

The model now includes paid-member growth as an explicit sensitivity input. The base 6% sales-growth and 7% fee-growth forecasts already reflect normal member growth; scenarios multiply both by the change in membership growth relative to the 6.3% base. That one-for-one link is a classroom judgment, not a measured company elasticity. The new sensitivity is in `COST_member_footprint_sensitivity.md`.
