## Initial peer policy — keep visible before calculating

A candidate may be used only if the **publicly traded parent whose P/E would be applied** earns most of its economics from a membership-warehouse model comparable with Costco's. The model must include all of the following:

1. recurring paid membership income that is a meaningful contributor to operating economics;
2. large-format warehouse clubs selling a broad, value-oriented assortment, including grocery/consumables and general merchandise, with comparable low-price and high-volume economics;
3. a material physical-club and e-commerce/omnichannel operation; and
4. positive, annual **GAAP diluted EPS** available publicly on or before August 31, 2026.

Differences are permissible only as **qualifications** to interpret a candidate's multiple: geography, club scale and maturity, gasoline exposure, merchandise mix, membership-fee contribution, renewal/retention, e-commerce mix, capital intensity, leverage, and growth runway. A candidate is excluded when the public parent's reported earnings and share price predominantly reflect another business model or when the available annual EPS is not comparable (for example, negative, materially nonrecurring, or not GAAP diluted).

This is an initial policy. Do not revise it after seeing prices or P/E multiples. If later evidence requires a revision, state the evidence and reason **before** recalculating.

## Evidence that would cause rejection before P/E calculation

### BJ's

Reject BJ's if primary-source evidence shows any of the following:

- membership income is no longer a meaningful contributor to its club economics, or the model is no longer primarily warehouse membership retail;
- gasoline, coupon/promotional activity, mix, or another disclosed item makes its annual GAAP EPS materially unrepresentative without a consistently supportable adjustment for both BJ's and Costco;
- leverage, capex/lease structure, unit-growth economics, or geographic concentration produces a risk/growth profile so different that the multiple would not be a reasonable Costco reference; or
- the August 31, 2026 price cannot be matched to an annual GAAP diluted-EPS period on a consistent trailing basis.

### Walmart / Sam's Club

The current evidence already supports exclusion. Reconsider only if primary disclosures provide a separately traded Sam's Club security or a reliable, stand-alone Sam's Club earnings-per-share measure that can be paired with that security's price. A segment revenue figure or WMT's consolidated EPS is insufficient.

## Skeptical-colleague review and source check

**Weakest supported assumption:** the DCF's 10.0% WACC. The saved DCF itself calls it a placeholder and unresolved because the 10-K does not provide a market cost of equity; it also reports terminal value as 73.4% of enterprise value. This makes the DCF particularly sensitive to an unsourced input.

**Mismatch check:**

- **Company / valuation object — reject as a criticism.** The DCF values enterprise cash flows and converts them to equity value by adding cash, subtracting debt, and dividing by diluted shares. The P/E uses an equity multiple and diluted EPS. Both outputs are Costco value per share, so they can be compared as cross-checks without averaging them.
- **Date — accept as a criticism.** The DCF document compares its value with a Sept. 9, 2026 quote of $902.75, while the peer P/E uses an Aug. 31, 2026 $943.89 close. This is a small but real date mismatch in the market-price comparisons. Before any conclusion is revised, align the displayed market comparison to one date or label the dates separately.
- **Earnings definition — unresolved.** COST's FY2025 and BJ's FY2025 figures are both annual, reported GAAP diluted EPS in U.S. dollars, so there is no GAAP-versus-adjusted or quarterly-versus-annual error. But Costco's fiscal year ended Aug. 31, 2025 and BJ's ended Jan. 31, 2026; earnings freshness and business conditions are not identical. The sources establish the dates but do not yet establish whether that timing difference is immaterial for the multiple.

**Question that could change the decision:** What primary-source or market-data evidence supports a defensible Costco WACC and normalized FCFF growth path that raises the DCF enough to create a margin of safety at the Aug. 31, 2026 price?

## Submission narrative

Costco's peer policy requires a publicly traded parent whose economics are principally recurring membership-warehouse retail: meaningful membership income, large-format low-price/high-volume clubs with grocery/consumables and general merchandise, a material club and omnichannel operation, and positive annual reported GAAP diluted EPS available by the valuation date. The policy permits qualifications for scale, geography, gasoline, mix, renewal, e-commerce, capital intensity, leverage, and growth; it excludes a public multiple when it mainly prices a different business.

I investigated two candidates. I marked **BJ's Wholesale Club (BJ) use, subject to review** because it is itself a membership-warehouse operator selling grocery, fresh foods, general merchandise, gasoline, ancillary services, and digital fulfillment to members. Its smaller, eastern-U.S.-concentrated footprint, gasoline exposure, and coupon/promotional model are qualifications to its multiple, not reasons to change the policy after seeing the result. I marked **Walmart/Sam's Club (WMT) exclude**. Sam's Club has relevant membership-club economics, but a WMT shareholder owns a much larger consolidated company: Sam's Club was 13% of FY2026 consolidated sales, and no stand-alone Sam's Club EPS or security is available. WMT's reported P/E therefore does not price a comparable stand-alone business. The primary-source links, section locators, annual reported EPS, and release dates are recorded above.

The checked peer comparison uses the August 31, 2026 COST and BJ closes and annual reported GAAP diluted EPS public by that date. BJ's $90.65 share price divided by its $4.38 FY2025 reported diluted EPS equals **20.696347x**. Applying that multiple to Costco's FY2025 reported diluted EPS of $18.21 yields **$376.88 per share**. This is a **single-peer reference**, not a defendable peer range, because BJ is the only admitted peer. Removing BJ predictably leaves no estimate; I did not remove it because its result is inconvenient.

The peer reference adds a market-based cross-check to the Week 3 DCF. The saved DCF produces **$263.20–$434.39 per share** across its disclosed WACC and terminal-growth sensitivity grid, with a **$324.54** base case. The P/E reference is above the DCF base case but sits within the saved DCF sensitivity range. The methods should not be mechanically averaged: the DCF discounts forecast free cash flow and converts enterprise value to equity value per share, while peer P/E applies how the market prices another company's reported per-share earnings. Both are equity values per Costco share, so they are comparable as cross-checks, but their assumptions and evidence differ.

The results differ primarily because the DCF uses an unresolved 10% WACC and a simplified 6.5583% five-year FCFF growth assumption, with terminal value accounting for 73.4% of enterprise value. The P/E result instead embeds the market's valuation of BJ, including BJ-specific growth, risk, capital structure, geography, and operating mix. The market-price comparisons also use different dates in the saved work: Sept. 9, 2026 for the DCF's displayed quote and Aug. 31, 2026 for P/E. I accepted the skeptical critique of the unsourced WACC and the date mismatch, rejected the claim that the methods value incompatible objects, and left fiscal-year timing comparability unresolved.

**Call: watch / defer; do not initiate now.** I can defend a DCF sensitivity range of **$263.20–$434.39** and a separate **$376.88 BJ single-peer reference**. I withhold a peer range because the policy currently admits only one public peer. Neither result supports averaging or an artificial precision point, and both are well below the saved market prices.

**Answer to the skeptical question:** No evidence in the current source set supports a market-based Costco WACC or normalized FCFF growth path sufficient to establish a margin of safety at the Aug. 31, 2026 price. The existing 10% WACC is explicitly a placeholder, so I will not claim a more favorable DCF merely by changing it. My decision could change if primary or market evidence supports a defensible WACC and a normalized FCFF forecast that materially improves the DCF under disclosed assumptions, or if a second policy-compliant membership-warehouse peer with same-date price and compatible annual GAAP diluted EPS produces a robust peer cross-check. Either development would be evaluated before changing the policy or recalculating.
